## 3. Free tier and churn evidence

### 3a. How this category structures free tiers (OBSERVED, see table for URLs)

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| No free tier, trial only | Obsidian Publish and Sync (no trial), Scribbles (14 days), Tinylytics (14 days), Plausible (30 days), Fathom, Blot, Micro.blog, Posthaven |
| Quota-based freemium | HackMD (1 MB images, 10 versions, 3-day trash), Pika (50 posts, 3 pages), Craft (1,500 blocks, 1 GB, no link sharing), Notion (5 MB uploads), Loom (25 videos, 5 min), Zight (1 GB, 5 min), Buttondown (100 subs), Neocities (1 GB), Excalidraw (1 scene), Typefully (10 posts/mo), mataroa (100 MB images) |
| Feature-gated freemium (custom domain is the paywall) | Bear Blog, mataroa, Write.as, Pika, Neocities |
| Fully free, donation or none | rentry, Gist, Simplenote, Telegraph, Carbon, Kap, Montaigne (custom domain free), Omnivore (dead) |

Custom domain is the single most common paid gate among the star benchmarks (Bear, mataroa, Write.as, Pika, Neocities, Montaigne charges $2/mo for it alone). Image hosting is the second (Write.as Pro "photo hosting", Pika "image upload", HackMD 1 MB vs 20 MB, mataroa 100 MB cap).

### 3b. Conversion and retention data

- RevenueCat, State of Subscription Apps 2025 (https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025): "Yearly plans significantly outperform monthly and weekly plans in 12-month retention across all categories, with some exceeding 50-60% retention, compared to 20-40% for monthly and under 10% for weekly." ### 3c. Churn causes for tiny subscriptions (what the data says)

- First-month cancellation of annual plans is the dominant leak (~30% in 2025, "over one third" in 2026, RevenueCat). Trials under 4 days convert worst (25.5% vs 42.5% for 17-32 day trials). "55% of all trial cancellations happen on Day 0." OBSERVED.
- Billing failures: "31% of all subscription cancellations" on Google Play and "only 14%" on App Store are billing errors (RevenueCat 2026). For a web checkout via Polar, card expiry on a yearly renewal is the analogous risk. OBSERVED.
- Indie Hackers, 2025-07-16 (https://www.indiehackers.com/post/subscriptions-vs-one-time-payments-a-developers-honest-take-f153e48960): "Churn is your silent killer: One quiet month and your MRR graph starts crying." Commenter: "I don't need this every week. I just want to use it when I'm doing a product launch". "Another monthly fee?" described as an "instant turnoff". OBSERVED.

### 4b. Yearly-only billing: for and against

For:
- Yearly retention beats monthly by 2.6x (44.1% vs 17.0%, RevenueCat 2025) and low-priced annual plans retain best of all (53.7%). Annual-first paywalls: 2.3x higher LTV (RevenueCat 2025 via search snippet https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025). OBSERVED/INFERRED.
- Direct category precedent for yearly-only at low prices: mataroa $9, omg.lol $20, 1Password Individual $35.88, HEY $99, Agenda $34.99. Users frame it as a domain renewal: omg.lol tells members to prepay "just like with any domain"; tredre3 compares to "an extra 10-20 bucks per year" for a domain.
- Sentiment: Groxx "The trend of everything being $5-10/month is ridiculous"; Indie Hackers "Another monthly fee?" is an "instant turnoff".
- Processor math (INFERRED from Polar fee schedule reported at https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/lemon-squeezy-vs-polar-paddle-merchant-of-record-2026 : "5% + $0.50 at the free Starter tier and 4.0% + $0.30 on the Growth plan"): on $8.99/year Polar keeps about $0.95 (10.6%). A hypothetical $0.99/month equivalent would lose about $0.55 per charge (56%). Below roughly $3/month, monthly billing is structurally unviable on a merchant-of-record; yearly-only is forced, not a preference.
- Baremetrics: annual "reduces support tickets by 12x" and involuntary churn "up to 95%". OBSERVED.

Against:
- Paddle (2021, OLD): annual-only "may aggravate potential buyers"; Baremetrics (2026): "Most SaaS companies offer both"; both recommend a 15-20% annual discount as the lever rather than removing monthly. Baremetrics notes monthly "lowers the barrier to entry and can drive initial conversion rates up by around 50%" for early-stage, individual-targeted products. OBSERVED.
- newscracker (2019, OLD): annual commitment "resulting in more loss aversion that prevents people from signing up for anything with a larger commitment next time."
- RevenueCat: ~30% to "over one third" of annual subscribers cancel auto-renew inside month one, so a yearly-only plan needs a strong first-month experience or the renewal is already lost.
- Annual share of app subscriptions fell from 41.4% to 33.6% year over year (RevenueCat 2026), i.e. the market is drifting toward monthly, not away from it.

Reconciliation: the "against" evidence is about $50 to $200/year SaaS where a monthly option is economically possible. At $8.99/year there is no viable monthly price, so the only real alternative to yearly-only is raising the price to $20+ and offering $2 to $3/month. Every star benchmark under $25/year (mataroa, omg.lol) is yearly-only; every one above $48/year offers monthly.

## 5. Synthesis and verdict

### Price clusters the evidence supports (individual, text publishing)

| Cluster | Yearly | Who | What it buys |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tip-sized | $9 to $10 | mataroa | Custom domain on a hobby project whose upkeep is ~€7/month and whose founder does not draw a salary; 3.7% of accounts paid (2022), a quarter of them never posted. |
| Domain-like identity fee | $20 to $40 | omg.lol ($20, moving to $50), Bear notes $29.99, Marked 3 $29.99, Paste $29.99, Agenda $34.99, 1Password $35.88, Ulysses $39.99; appopportunity guidance "$20-$40/year" | The dominant indie-utility price. Users describe $20/yr as "very reasonable" and compare it to a domain renewal. omg.lol found $20 could not fund full-time work and is moving to $50. |
| Hosting | $48 to $96 | Obsidian Sync Standard $48, Bear Blog $49, Pika $60, Write.as $72, Scribbles $90, Standard Notes $90, Obsidian Publish $96 | Custom domain, images, multiple sites. Contested: $60 is "fair enough" to one HN user and "very steep" to another in the same thread; Obsidian Publish at $96 is the canonical "quite expensive". |

Seshat Share as described (push .md, public link, update in place, dashboard, no custom domain, images probably paid) sits functionally between rentry (free) and mataroa ($9). Its hosting cost per user is near zero for text (Bunny CDN $0.01/GB, $1/month minimum; Bear Blog: "low is not zero").

### Verdict on $8.99/year

1. It is defensible and has one exact-match precedent (mataroa $9), and it is the price the loudest sentiment in this category asks for ("everything being $5-10/month is ridiculous"). It also maximizes retention per RevenueCat: low-priced annual plans are the single best-retaining SKU in the data (53.7% after one year).

2. It is a supporter price, not a business price. The only benchmark at this level is run as a hobby with ~€7/month upkeep, and its buyers partly pay out of goodwill (24% never posted). omg.lol, the closest "identity plus page" analogue, concluded $20/year was not enough and is moving to $50. Bear Blog's revenue only became meaningful at $5/month. RevenueCat's median first-year realized LTV per payer for low-priced apps is $10.69, which is roughly what $8.99 yields after Polar's ~$0.95 fee.

3. Yearly-only is the correct structure at this price and needs no apology: below about $3/month a monthly SKU is eaten by the merchant-of-record's fixed per-transaction fee, and every sub-$25/year product in the category is yearly-only. The anti-annual-only literature applies to $50+ SaaS with a viable monthly option.

4. Free tier: the category consensus is quota-based freemium with custom domain and images as the paid gates. A 3 to 5 active-link free tier with no images matches Pika (50 posts), Loom (25 videos), HackMD (1 MB images) in spirit. Bear Blog's experience says the paid tier also functions as a spam filter for public-publishing free tiers, which matters for seshat.page slugs.

5. Lifetime: no evidence supports a lifetime tier at this price. Category lifetimes run 2.5x to 4x annual, which would be $22 to $36, an amount that would read as a joke next to Bear Blog's $189 and would lock in server cost forever. If a lifetime is ever offered it should follow a price rise to the $20-40 cluster and be set at 3-4x (roughly $79 to $119, matching Agenda $119.99 and Bear Blog $189 to $199).

6. Recommended path given the evidence: keep $8.99/year as the launch and early-bird price with an explicit grandfather promise (Bear 2 and Obsidian Publish show this defuses later increases), and plan the standard price for the $19 to $29 cluster once images, private/expiring links, or custom domain ship. If custom domain ships, the benchmarks (Bear $49, Pika $60) say $29 to $49 is the ceiling before "steep" comments appear. Do not add monthly billing until the price is at least $24/year ($2/month), where the Polar fee falls under 25% of a monthly charge.

Confidence: moderate. Price points are almost all first-party and current. Willingness-to-pay quotes skew HN and Obsidian forum because Reddit was unreachable; about half are older than 24 months and are flagged. No survey data specific to markdown publishing was found; queries tried are listed in section 2.
